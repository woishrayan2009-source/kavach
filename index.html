One safety brain.
Eight hazards.

Fire, electrical faults, break-ins, medical emergencies, falls, women's safety, floods, and school safety are each being solved today by separate, disconnected gadgets that don't talk to each other. That's the real problem — not that any one hazard is unsolved, but that solving it in isolation means a family or institution ends up managing eight different apps, eight different alert chains, and eight blind spots where those chains don't connect. Kavach's contribution isn't a new sensor — it's proving that all eight can report into one dashboard and one shared alert-escalation cascade, like a shield made of many plates acting as a single layer of armor.

Electrical

Electrical Fault Early-Warning

Concept

Concept only — no hardware or software built yet.

Problem

Electrical short-circuits are India's single largest cause of urban fires, and most safety projects only react after flame or smoke — by which point the fault has already ignited something.

Solution

A current-sensing module that watches for the erratic signature of arcing and cuts power to that circuit before it reaches ignition temperature.

In full

In Delhi alone, roughly 70% of fire calls are attributed to electrical short-circuiting, and fire-safety engineers note that micro-arcing — a hazardous spark inside wiring, distinct from a full short — causes the large majority of these before any flame is visible. Arc-Fault Circuit Interrupters that catch this early have been mandatory in U.S. homes since 1999 but are essentially absent from Indian residential wiring codes. The proposed module clips a non-invasive current transformer onto a circuit, distinguishes arcing's erratic signature from a normal overload, and reports the fault to the Kavach dashboard the same way Extingo reports a confirmed fire.

Why it strengthens Kavach

It's the one module that acts before a fire starts, not after — a meaningfully different value proposition from every other module in the grid.

Women's Safety

Panic Trigger

Concept

Concept only — no hardware or software built yet.

Problem

Manual panic-button wearables require the wearer to be conscious, free, and able to physically press a button — precisely the condition that's often not met in a real assault.

Solution

A low-cost heart-rate and accelerometer anomaly detector as an automatic secondary trigger, alongside the manual button, so the alert can still fire even if the wearer can't act.

In full

Manual panic-button wearables are already a very common category, but their core weakness is that they stop at "press this button." The proposed change is a sudden, sharp heart-rate spike combined with irregular, forceful motion — an automatic secondary trigger that reports into the same Kavach alert cascade (SMS, call, live location) already proven in Extingo and Rakshak, rather than existing as a standalone gadget.

Why frame it this way

Presenting it as "we know this category is common, here's specifically what we changed and why" is far stronger in front of judges than presenting it as if it were novel outright.

Flood

Water-Level Sensing

Concept

Concept only — no hardware or software built yet.

Problem

Sudden waterlogging and flash flooding in flood-prone towns often catches residents unaware, because there's no local early-warning layer beneath river-basin-scale forecasting.

Solution

An ultrasonic water-level sensor at a known reference height that reports rate-of-rise to the dashboard and triggers a local alarm plus SMS once water crosses a safety threshold.

In full

A drain, basement entrance, or low-lying street point gets an ultrasonic sensor mounted at a known reference height, reporting rate-of-rise the same way Extingo's heat sensor already does — just reapplied to a different hazard.

Why it strengthens Kavach

It reuses the same rate-of-rise algorithm concept from Extingo — one idea, three hazards: heat, water, and by extension gas concentration.

School Safety

Child Safety Tracking

Concept

Concept only — no hardware or software built yet.

Problem

Parents often have no confirmation a child was safely dropped at school or picked up — most school transport systems still rely on manual attendance.

Solution

An RFID tap-in/tap-out system at the school gate and bus door that notifies a parent's phone the moment a child taps in or out.

In full

Beyond the tap notification itself, the system raises an alert to the Kavach dashboard if a child who tapped onto the morning bus never taps in at school within an expected time window — catching the case a simple notification would miss.

Why it strengthens Kavach

It extends the safety theme beyond the home into the school, broadening the project's relevance for the exhibition's theme.

Command Grid

Unified Dashboard

Concept

Concept only — no hardware or software built yet.

Problem

Every module above, on its own, is a point solution. None of them talk to each other, and a family or institution would need eight separate apps.

Solution

One dashboard where every module reports its status, and one shared alert-escalation chain — local alarm, SMS, Telegram with snapshot and location, automated call — that every module reuses.

Why this is the actual thesis

This is the connective idea behind the whole project: not eight products, one grid. See the Architecture page for the full diagram of how each module feeds into this shared cascade.
